<details name="chapter" open>
<summary>Problem detected</summary>

Young urban travellers don't find Antwerp relevant. They know it exists but nothing has made them feel like it was made for them. The city's most interesting qualities are invisible in conventional tourism content

</details>

<details name="chapter">
<summary>The research question</summary>

How might we reshape the perception of Antwerp for young urban travellers, shifting their mindset from ”Go to Antwerp? No thanks!” to seeing it as a vibrant, must-visit destination?

</details>

<details name="chapter">
<summary>experience it</summary>

A digital-first platform that lets the city speak for itself. Sounds Like Antwerp turns Antwerp's real sensor data into generative music and art. This gives anyone the opportunity to experience the city before they set any foot in it. It not just gives them a reason to go visit it, but also to feel like they are part of it.

</details>

<details name="chapter">
<summary>Technology</summary>

The experience is built around the idea of transforming real-time city data into generative music and visuals. Different data points, such as location, movement, temperature, noise and other urban signals, are translated into musical parameters like tempo, rhythm, intensity and sound selection.
The result is a soundtrack that changes depending on where and when you experience Antwerp.

The prototype was developed using a combination of code and no-code tools. Custom code handles the data logic and generative behaviour, while the no-code setup makes it possible to connect the different elements into an interactive experience without building a complete production platform from scratch.

This allowed us to quickly test the concept, experiment with different data inputs and see how Antwerp could translate into sound.

</details>


<details name="chapter">
<summary>Proof of concept</summary>

Sounds Like Antwerp was developed as a proof of concept for Visit Antwerp, rather than a fully production-ready platform.

The goal was to demonstrate how the idea could work, what the user experience could look like and how technology could bring the concept to life. Because this was developed within a project context for a real client, the prototype focuses on concept, interaction and feasibility rather than a complete technical infrastructure.
The prototype shows the potential of the idea,  not the final product.

</details>
